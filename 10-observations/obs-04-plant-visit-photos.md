## 1. Traceability — Pyramid Serialises Individual Units 🟢

The single most consequential finding for Phlo.

| Marking | Value observed | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit serial** | `PTL-VII-L1-26-H-3493` | `PTL` = Pyramid Technoplast Ltd · `VII` = **Unit 7** · `L1` = **Line 1** · `26` = year 2026 · `H` = month code (8th letter = August) · `3493` = sequence |
| **UN certification** | `31HA1/Y/0826` | `31` = composite IBC for liquids · `HA1` = rigid plastic inner, steel outer · `Y` = packing groups II/III · `0826` = **manufactured August 2026** |
| **Approval ref** | `SGP/PMD0017358U/1960` | Certification body reference and stacking load |
| **Max gross** | `1360 kg max` | Moulded into the bottle |
| **QC pass** | Orange **"TESTED OK"** sticker | Applied per unit |
| **Machine asset tag** | `PTL/BM/01` | Blow Moulding machine 01 — assets are numbered |

**Every IBC carries a unique, decodable serial identifying its unit, line, month and sequence.**
Pyramid already has per-unit traceability at the point of manufacture. Phlo does not need to invent
a serialisation scheme — it needs to *capture the one that exists*, and that scheme already encodes
the unit/line/period structure a production module would need.

🟠 (JB): if the sequence is per line per year, **Line 1 at Unit 7 had produced 3,493 IBCs by
August 2026**. That is the first production-volume figure in the project. It needs confirming — the
counter may reset monthly.

## 2. The Quality System Is Formal, Documented and Live 🟢

Photographed: a binder of **"MASTER COPY / CONTROLLED COPY"** Quality System Work Instructions on
Pyramid Technoplast Ltd letterhead. This is an ISO-style controlled document set.

**Common header on every document:** Reference No · Revision no `01` · Effective Date `01.07.2023` ·
Next Revision Date `01.07.2026` · Page No · Prepared By `DEP (HOD)/MR` · Approved By `Plant (HOD)`.

⚠️ **The next revision date has passed.** Every instruction shows `01.07.2026` as its review date;
the visit was 2026-08-20. Either a review is overdue, or a newer revision exists that was not on the
board.

### Documents identified

| Ref | Title | Content observed |
|---|---|---|
| `PTL/WI/PD/04` | **Granulation** | Granulating surplus flash, cut pieces, test pieces and rejected trims back into raw material |
| `PTL/WI/PD/05` | **Workmanship and Closure Fitment & Accessories Fitment** | Surface finish, shade, embossment legibility, final finishing, sealing notch, handle/lug finishing, closure fitment |
| `PTL/WI/PD/07` | **Product Startup / Process Stabilization & Online Approval of Product** | Trial run, cooling, leak test, defect criteria, NCP handling, handover to QA |
| *(unnumbered)* | **Blow moulding machine parameter setting** | Process control parameters, mould mounting, dry cycle |
| `PTL/QA/8.5/F-06` | In-process Inspection report | Referenced as a record |
| `CP-002` | Control Plan | Referenced |

### Blow moulding process parameters 🟢 (transcribed)

- Temperature of all zones **set with respect to MFI of the selected material**
- Actual temperature tolerance **within 10% of set temperature**
- **Pre-blow pressure: 4 to 6.5 kgf/cm²**
- **Main blow pressure: 2 to 3.5 kgf/cm²**
- Load current, hydraulic and pneumatic pressure gauges, temperature controller — all monitored
- Colour/appearance **matched visually against a shade card / cut piece / approved sample kept near the machine**
- After production, **date, shift and product number are marked on the product**
- Inputs listed: blow moulding machine, compressed air, cooling water, chilled water, **approved HDPE material**, grinder, trained operator

### Mould handling 🟢

Identification in mould store → check last inspection & repair status → clean with cotton waste
until free of oil/grease → check for logos (India logo, **UN number**, bung housing, day/date/year
dial) → mount and connect hydraulic, pneumatic, limit/proximity switches, cooling water → verify
open/close of mould and slides → run a **dry cycle** in auto mode.

### Leak test — exact specification 🟢

> "It is applicable only for blow moulded containers (**210 ltr and above capacity**)… Apply pressure
> of **300 millibar** inside the barrel… The pressure is observed for **12 seconds** after attaining
> 300 millibar. Permissible reduction in pressure is **25% (i.e. up to 190 millibar)** and green lamp
> will glow, indicating passes the test." Sudden pressure fall → red lamp → container rejected.

### Reject handling 🟢

Foreign particles, pin holes → container **rejected, its serial number deleted from the production
record**, and sent to the granulating machine. Oil/water present → cleaned and sent to **NCP
(Non-Conforming Product) area**, recorded on the production sheet.

**Rejects are already tracked against serial numbers and removed from production counts.** There is
an existing data discipline here that the ERP does not appear to hold.

### Visual defect standards — three separate boards 🟢

Large printed accept/reject photo-matrices mounted at the point of work:

1. **"Visual Defects Acceptance Criteria for Inner Container"** — IC in dusty condition, dent top portion, knife cut mark at filling opening, water mark, black spot, thread cut at filling opening, thread damage at discharge opening, improper finishing, dust mark, thread cut at discharge opening, scratches on body, knife cut.
2. **"Visual Defects Acceptance Criteria for Grid, Cage & IBC"** — mounted in the cage assembly area.
3. **"Visual Defects Acceptance Criteria for Bulk Packaging"** — the largest, ~28 defect types for drums: shade variation, dent, drainage hole formation, scratch mark, black spot on body, air bubble, oil mark, fiber inside on the body, thread cut/poor thread formation, water mark, melt fracture, dent & flow mark on top surface near top weld-line, folding mark, flash/burrs after drainage hole, poor seat formation, bung house ovality, flash on XI-ring, sealing hole block, flash on partline, sealing notch cut, bung house collar cut, patches on the surface, bung house bend during cooling, embossing visuality, company name, flow mark & flash on top surface, white mark on belly.

Also observed: **"WATERBATH LEAKAGE TEST"** station signage, a **"SOP DISPLAY"** board, and Hindi
machine operating instructions at the panel.

## 3. The IBC Bill of Materials — resolves open question 10.14 🟢

The IBC is assembled almost entirely in-house. Only the fittings are bought in.

| Component | Source | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner bottle** | **In-house** — blow moulded HDPE | Moulding machine `PTL/BM/01`, bottles on the line, dedicated QC board |
| **Steel tube** | **In-house** — made from strip on a tube mill | Tube mill / roll-forming line with "High Efficiency Switch Welder"; bundles of finished galvanised round and profiled tube |
| **Cage / grid** | **In-house** — cut, bent, clinched/welded | **"IBC Grid Production Line"** (IBC 框架生产流水线), supplied by **Ningbo Xinfeng Welding Equipment Co. Ltd**, Ningbo, Zhejiang, China. Labelled locally as **"CHINCHING M/C"** |
| **Pallet base** | **In-house** — pressed steel with plastic corner feet | Rows of finished galvanised bases; punching presses **BNX 100** and **BNX 160** |
| **Butterfly valve** | **IMPORTED** — China | Warehouse of cartons: *Qingdao XiFa Plastic Products Co., Ltd*, marked **"Butterfly Valve"** with specification and quantity fields |
| **Cam lock** | **IMPORTED** — China | Same supplier, cartons marked **"Cam Lock"** |
| **Caps / closures** | `[UNKNOWN]` | Not separately identified |

**The answer to 10.14 is: the cage is in-house, and so is the tube it is made from.** Vertical
integration runs deeper than anyone in this project assumed — Pyramid buys steel strip and makes its
own tube. What it buys in are the **valves and cam locks, imported from XiFa in Qingdao**, held in
substantial pallet-stacked inventory.

🟠 (JB): imported fittings held in that volume implies long lead times and large safety stock —
plausibly a meaningful share of the trapped working capital, and a Path A/Path B question in its own
right (who buys them?).

## 4. Raw Material — resin is multi-source, and partly domestic 🟢

**This corrects the 2026-08-18 finding.** The website states the plastic lines run on *"imported HDPE
granules from Marlex HXM TR-571"*. The material actually on the floor tells a broader story:

| Brand | Producer | Origin | Pack |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SABIC** | SABIC | **Made in Saudi Arabia** (Arabic script on bag) | 25 kg net |
| **PROPEL** | **Indian Oil Corporation Limited, Panipat, Haryana** | **Made in India** — ISI marked | 25 kg |
| *Marlex* | Chevron Phillips | Per website only | Not seen in the photographs |

**So resin is dual-sourced: imported (SABIC, Saudi) and domestic (IOCL Propel, India).** The
inference drawn on 2026-08-18 — that importing explains why Path A is "sensitive" and why lead times
are long — **is weakened**. A domestic IOCL supply has short lead times, no customs, no LC and no FX.

## 5. Granulation and Recycling — partially resolves 10.5 🟢

`PTL/WI/PD/04` documents an **in-house granulation loop**:

> Purpose: *"To granulate the surplus flash, cut pieces, test pieces and rejected trims material
> generated in process."* Scope: *"applicable to all rejected & tested container body in house."*
> Responsibility: **Shift Engineer**.
> Definition — *Granulating material:* "the surplus, tested and rejected material is granulated **as
> raw material**." *Cut piece:* "a part of product having manageable size which can be fed [to the]
> machine."
> Procedure: rejected and tested product is **cut into small pieces manually** → cut pieces stored in
> **plastic open-top drums** to avoid dirt/damage → fed to the granulating machine → granulated
> material collected from the outlet.

Photographed: the **granulating machine** with a hopper of white HDPE regrind, and a large floor
stock of **used, dirty returned drums** awaiting reprocessing.

**Steel scrap is also collected** — bulk bags of coiled offcuts and punching swarf from the tube and
press lines.

This is in-plant regrind, distinct from the separate **recycling plant** named in recording 1 — that
site is still unvisited and unexplained.

> *"We have IBC recollect service in following countries"* — Albania, Australia, Belgium, Bosnia,
> Bulgaria, Croatia, Estonia, Europe, Finland, France, Germany, Hungary, Indonesia, Latvia,
> Luxembourg, Malaysia, Middle East, Moldova, Montenegro, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway,
> Philippines, Poland, Romania, Saudi Arabia, Serbia, Singapore, Slovakia, Slovenia, South America,
> Sweden, Thailand, UK, USA.
>
> Contact `ecocollect@pyramidtechnoplast.com` · `www.pyramidibccontainers.com`

Also photographed: a forklift **loading shrink-wrapped IBCs into a shipping container**.

**Pyramid exports IBCs to roughly forty countries and operates an international take-back
programme.** No observation, process map, gap analysis or PRD in this project mentions exports at
all. The signals were there in hindsight — the sales invoice has an *Export Type* field, RODTEP sits
in the glossary, and the company holds an EcoVadis rating — but nobody connected them.

**Implications:** export documentation, shipping bills, letters of credit, FX realisation, RODTEP
claims, container booking and freight forwarding are all live processes that are entirely unmapped —
and a dedicated export domain (`pyramidibccontainers.com`) suggests a distinct commercial channel.

## 7. Screen Printing Is In-House — corrects a job-work hypothesis 🟢

Photographed: workers applying **yellow screen-printing frames** by hand to blue 210 L drums.

A finished drum reads:

```
SUPPLIER NAME:- HEENA SALES CORPORATION
               403, AN ANTA STALLION, SAVASI-BARODA
PRODUCT :- FORMALDEHYDE 37%
MFGR'S NAME :- SIMALIN CHEMICAL INDUSTRIES PVT. LTD.
               13, GIDC, NANDESARI, BARODA
GROSS WEIGHT : 197.80 KG.
TARE WEIGHT  :   7.80 KG.
NET WEIGHT   : 190.00 KG.
```

Three things follow:

1. **Screen printing is done in-house, manually.** On 2026-08-19 it was hypothesised as a likely job-work operation because the Sales Invoice carries a *"Screen Charges"* line-item. That charge is for an in-house service, not a subcontracted one. **The job-work question (10.21/10.23) needs a different answer.**
2. **Pyramid prints its customer's customer's data.** The drum names the filler (Heena Sales), the chemical (Formaldehyde 37%) and the chemical manufacturer (Simalin). This is deep make-to-order: each print run is specific to a customer *and their product*.
3. **`TARE WEIGHT : 7.80 KG` is the drum's own weight** — which confirms, definitively, that the *weight* attribute in the 448-SKU item master (NMD-210 at 8.0/8.5/9.0/9.5/10.0/10.5 KG) is the **physical drum weight / wall-thickness grade**, and that it is a customer-facing, printed specification rather than an internal code.

## Open Questions

1. **Which town is Unit VII?** The serial confirms the unit number, not the location.
2. **Does the serial sequence reset monthly or annually?** Determines whether 3,493 is a monthly or year-to-date figure — and whether it is the project's first real volume number.
3. **Is the serial captured anywhere digitally,** or only marked on the unit and written on the production sheet?
4. **Where does the production sheet live?** It is referenced repeatedly in the work instructions but was not photographed. It is probably the single most important undocumented record in the plant.
5. **Are the Quality System Work Instructions overdue for revision,** or is a current set held elsewhere?
6. **What share of resin is imported (SABIC) vs domestic (IOCL Propel)?** Reframes the Path A "sensitive" question.
7. **Who procures the imported valves and cam locks** — promoters (Path A) or purchase team (Path B)?
8. **How large is the export business,** and what share of revenue? Which countries actually ship?
9. **How does the IBC RECOLLECT programme operate** — who collects, who pays, what comes back to India?
10. **What happens to returned used drums?** Reconditioned and resold, or granulated?
11. **If screen printing is in-house, what is actually job-worked** under Labour Job Issue III and IV?
12. **Is galvanising done in-house or sent out?** Tube and cages are galvanised; no galvanising line was photographed.
13. **How many lines are there per unit?** The serial says `L1` — implying at least a Line 2 somewhere.
14. **Are the three visual-defect boards backed by written specs,** or are the photo-matrices the standard?
